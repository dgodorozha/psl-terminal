0POLPolicy library: instruments, your own, assumptions
The instrument library, and your own instruments.
  1. Every built in instrument is a bounded JSON document; the table shows what each does. Name instruments in the policy question to compare them, or leave it to the default battery.
  2. To define your own, edit the document on the left: scope, phase in, threshold, costs, mandated safety share, evaluation catch rate, delays, liability, transparency, enforcement, controls. Every value is clamped to the bounds on the right and clamping is reported.
  3. Click Validate and store. The instrument joins every later plan while it is enabled, with its author and hash in the battery hash and the ledger.
  4. Assumption overrides (bottom) apply to the next plan: any value judgement in the assumptions table, within bounds.
Nothing simulates until the battery is approved at Gate 1.

DDATData desk: price series through quarantine
Bring a price series into the Lab, through quarantine.
  1. Choose the classification: public, synthetic, licensed or pseudonymised. It decides what may leave the run directory.
  2. Drop a CSV or JSON file with a date column and a price column (optionally a market column for a beta). Daily or monthly; the Lab keeps the last observation of each month.
  3. Optionally give an event month (YYYY-MM, a past regulatory announcement): the cumulative abnormal return over the six months after it is computed and compared in assurance with the model's early reaction.
  4. Optionally map the series to a firm archetype by name: its volatility and beta calibrate that firm in the next plan.
  5. Every file passes one quarantine: size and type limits, a schema check, a formula injection check, a personal identifier scan that refuses rather than redacts, and a hash for the ledger. Rows never enter the briefing or the evidence pack.

AAGTFirm workshop: archetypes and templates
The firm archetypes, and your own.
  1. The default economy is eight archetypes of the AI value chain (below). None is a real company.
  2. To replace it, define at least two firms: revenue, margin, growth, R&D share, capability, safety practice, reach, the share of revenue in the regulated scope, mobility offshore, safety culture, beta, volatility and multiples. Every value is bounded.
  3. A firm can be calibrated from a price series uploaded in DAT: map the dataset to the firm's name and its volatility and beta replace the template's in the next plan.
  4. Click Validate and store; enabled templates replace the default economy in the next plan when there are at least two.
The engine is agnostic to names: a mapping onto real companies is the authority's, and the briefing says so.

PSL▌Policy Scenario Lab

An agent based laboratory for AI regulation: candidate instruments run against the same synthetic AI economy, and are scored on the harm they prevent against the economic and social cost they impose, with the industry's gain or loss, the news to expect and the market reaction beside them.

From the team behind the Herding Scenario Lab. For policy, supervision and impact assessment teams.

NWSnews wire: the headlines four media agents write from the simulation, live, and the forecast by quarter1
MKTtechnology equities: every firm's price under the instrument being simulated, and the index against the baseline2
SAFsafety forecast: incidents, the probability of a major and of a catastrophic one, harm, safety practice, trust3
PNLindustry gain or loss: each firm's value change against the baseline, with its probability of gaining4
WLFsocial ledger: what the instrument buys and costs, as a waterfall, and who gains and who pays5
GAPdecision gap: the instruments ranked by how they look early against how they score over the horizon6
LDGrationale ledger: the signed hash chain, entry by entry, with replay7
